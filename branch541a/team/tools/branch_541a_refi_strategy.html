The DPA second lien is the refi wrinkle most LOs miss
When you refinance a DPA borrower, the second lien doesn't disappear. It must either be paid off, subordinated, or forgiven. Each program handles this differently — and getting it wrong kills the refi. Know these rules cold before you quote a single DPA borrower a refi rate.
Second lien treatment by DPA program — refi scenario
DPA Program Second lien type Refi treatment Timeline consideration Difficulty
NV Home Is Possible (HIP) Forgivable 3yr If 3 years passed → second is forgiven, clean refi. If under 3 years → second must subordinate or be repaid. Wait for 3-year forgiveness mark. Most rate drop scenarios hit after 3 years anyway. Easy after year 3
Chenoa Fund Repayable 10yr 0% CBC Mortgage must approve subordination. Chenoa generally allows resubordination. Second stays in place behind new first. Need to request subordination agreement from CBC. Add 1–2 weeks to timeline. Manageable — CBC cooperative
NHF National Grant — no second No second lien. Cleanest refi in the DPA portfolio. Standard FHA Streamline or conventional refi — no subordination needed. No delay — process like any standard refi. Zero friction
FL Housing / FL Assist Deferred — due on sale/refi Technically due at refi. Borrower must repay FL Assist second at closing OR program may allow subordination. Check current FL Housing policy. Borrower needs enough equity to absorb payoff. Critical to model equity position before quoting. Verify with FL Housing first
FL Hometown Heroes Deferred — due on sale/refi Same as FL Assist — technically due at refi event. Borrower must have equity to absorb or program subordination applies. Model equity carefully. $35K second on a $350K purchase = 10% — must be absorbed or subordinated. Verify with FL Housing first
TSAHC (TX) Grant option Grant version = no second, clean refi. Deferred second version = subordination required through TSAHC servicer. If borrower took grant option — zero friction refi. If deferred second — allow 2 weeks for sub agreement. Easy if grant was used
TDHCA (TX) Deferred second lien Must be subordinated or paid off. TDHCA has a subordination process — not instantaneous. Allow 3–4 weeks for TDHCA subordination. Plan timeline accordingly. Manageable with lead time
AZ Home in Five Grant — no second No second lien. Same as NHF — completely clean refi with zero subordination complexity. No delay. Zero friction
CO CHFA Deferred second CHFA second due on refi unless CHFA approves subordination. CHFA does allow subordination requests. Allow 2–3 weeks for CHFA subordination approval. Manageable
The highest-risk refi scenario in your portfolio: FL Housing deferred second (FL Assist or Hometown Heroes) where the borrower has minimal equity. The $10,000–$35,000 second lien is technically due at refi. Before you quote any FL DPA borrower a refi, model their equity position first. If they're thin on equity, the refi may not be feasible until they have enough appreciation to absorb the second payoff.
Best refi scenario in your portfolio: NHF grant borrowers (no second lien) and HIP forgivable borrowers past year 3 (forgiven second). These files close fast, clean, and with zero subordination drama. Sales Boomerang can tag these clients by program type in Total Expert so you know exactly which ones to call first when rates move.
Rate drop playbook — what branch 541-A does the day rates move
1
Sales Boomerang fires the alert — you don't have to watch rates manually
Sales Boomerang Rate Watch is already monitoring your Total Expert database. The moment a client's existing rate becomes refinanceable by a meaningful margin, you get an alert. This is already included in your PRMG branch MarTech stack at zero additional cost. Make sure every closed DPA borrower is loaded into Total Expert at closing — not later, not eventually. 2
Segment your portfolio by DPA program before you call anyone
Tag every closed file in Total Expert by DPA program. When rates drop, your first calls are NHF grant borrowers and HIP forgivable post-year-3 borrowers — zero subordination friction, fastest closings. FL Housing deferred borrowers go in a second batch pending equity review. This segmentation takes 2 hours to set up today and saves chaos when rates actually move.
3
FHA Streamline first — 72-hour qualification, minimal docs
FHA Streamline closings can move in 21–30 days with a cooperative title company. No appraisal. No income verification. The bottleneck is Gissella's pipeline, not docs. When a refi wave hits, temporarily pause new purchase intakes at a safe threshold and redirect processing capacity to streamlines. FHA Streamline refis are faster to close than DPA purchase files — use that speed.
4
Get subordination agreements started on day one for deferred second clients
For Chenoa, TDHCA, CHFA, and FL Housing borrowers — don't wait until mid-process to request subordination. Contact the second lien servicer the same day you lock the rate. These agreements take 2–4 weeks. Starting late means rate locks expire and borrowers get frustrated. Build a subordination request template for each program and fire it immediately upon application.
5
Use the refi as a referral trigger — not just a transaction
When you call a past DPA borrower to offer a rate reduction, you are reactivating a relationship that was probably dormant. That call produces three outcomes: (1) a refi closing, (2) a referral to a family member or friend who also wants a home, and (3) a Google review request that BirdEye automatically sends post-close. One phone call, three revenue opportunities. This is how the DPA portfolio compounds beyond just rate savings.
